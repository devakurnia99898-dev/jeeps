Both modifications require careful consideration of the vehicle's overall system, including the transmission, cooling system, and suspension.

## Technical Deep Dive
To understand how turbocharging and engine swapping work, it's essential to dive into the technical details of these modifications. Turbocharging involves the use of a turbocharger, which is a turbine-driven forced induction device that compresses air and forces it into the engine's intake system. The turbocharger is powered by the engine's exhaust gases, which spin a turbine and drive a compressor.

Engine swapping, on the other hand, involves replacing the vehicle's original engine with a more powerful one. This requires careful consideration of the vehicle's transmission, cooling system, and suspension, as well as the engine's mounting points and wiring harness.

Common failure points in turbocharging and engine swapping include the turbocharger's bearings and seals, the engine's piston rings and cylinders, and the transmission's gears and bearings. It's essential to identify these potential failure points and take steps to mitigate them.
