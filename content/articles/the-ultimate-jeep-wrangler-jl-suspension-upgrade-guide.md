## Technical Deep Dive
The Jeep Wrangler (JL) suspension system consists of a combination of coil springs, shocks, and control arms. The coil springs provide the primary suspension travel, while the shocks dampen the oscillations and control the rate of suspension compression and rebound. The control arms, including the upper and lower control arms, locate the axle and provide additional stability and articulation.

Common failure points in the stock suspension system include:

* Weak or worn-out coil springs, which can cause sagging or loss of ride height
* Insufficiently valved or worn-out shocks, which can lead to harsh ride quality or excessive vibration
* Loose or worn-out control arm bushings, which can cause excessive play or instability in the suspension

## Step-by-Step Guide

#### Step 1: Remove Stock Coil Springs
Remove the stock coil springs by compressing them using a coil spring compressor and then removing the retaining clips.

#### Step 2: Install New Coil Springs
Install the new coil springs, ensuring they are properly seated and aligned.

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes
* Always follow proper torque specs when tightening suspension components.
* Use a torque wrench to ensure accurate and consistent torque values.
* Never over-tighten suspension components, as this can cause damage or premature wear.
* Always test the suspension system after upgrading to ensure proper function and performance.
