### **Safety Warnings**
When working with electrical systems, it's essential to disconnect the battery and ensure the vehicle is in park. When working with suspension systems, it's crucial to use proper lifting techniques and support the vehicle with jack stands.

## **Step-by-Step Guide**
### **Step 1: Prepare Your Garage**
#### **Step 1.1: Clear the Workspace**
Clear the workspace of any clutter or obstacles. Ensure the floor is clean and dry, and the area is well-lit.

#### **Step 1.2: Gather Tools and Equipment**
Gather the necessary tools and equipment, including a 10mm socket, torque wrench, pliers, screwdrivers, and multimeter.

### **Step 2: Inspect the Vehicle**
#### **Step 2.1: Check the Engine**
Check the engine for any signs of wear or damage. Look for leaks, corrosion, or excessive wear on the belts and hoses.

#### **Step 2.2: Check the Suspension**
Check the suspension for any signs of wear or damage. Look for leaks, corrosion, or excessive wear on the shocks and springs.

### **Step 3: Perform Routine Maintenance**
#### **Step 3.1: Change the Oil**
Change the oil and filter according to the manufacturer's recommendations. Use a high-quality oil filter and the correct type of oil for the vehicle.

#### **Step 3.2: Check the Brakes**
Check the brakes for any signs of wear or damage. Look for excessive wear on the pads, rotors, or drums.

## **Pro Tips & Common Mistakes**
* Don't forget to disconnect the battery before starting work on the electrical system.
* Use proper lifting techniques when working with suspension systems.
* Don't overtighten the bolts and screws, as this can cause damage to the threads.
