## Technical Deep Dive
The Jeep Avenger features a 1.5L turbocharged engine, which provides an impressive balance of power and fuel efficiency. The engine is paired with a 6-speed manual transmission or a 7-speed dual-clutch automatic transmission, depending on the trim level. The Avenger also features a MacPherson strut front suspension and a torsion beam rear suspension, which provides a smooth and comfortable ride.

One of the common failure points in the Avenger's engine is the turbocharger. The turbocharger is responsible for compressing air and forcing it into the engine's combustion chamber, which increases power output. However, the turbocharger can be prone to failure due to excessive heat, oil contamination, or foreign object damage. Regular maintenance, such as oil changes and filter replacements, can help prevent turbocharger failure.

Another critical component of the Avenger's suspension is the coil springs. The coil springs are responsible for absorbing bumps and shocks, providing a smooth ride and stable handling. However, the coil springs can be prone to wear and tear, which can lead to a decrease in ride quality and handling performance. Regular inspections and replacements can help prevent coil spring failure.

## Step-by-Step Guide
### Introduction to the Guide
In this section, we'll provide a step-by-step guide on how to prep your Jeep Avenger for optimal performance. We'll cover essential maintenance tasks, such as oil changes and filter replacements, as well as more advanced procedures, such as turbocharger inspections and coil spring replacements.

### Oil Change
#### Draining the Oil
1. Locate the oil drain plug, which is usually located at the bottom of the engine.
2. Use a 10mm socket to loosen the oil drain plug.
3. Allow the oil to drain into a pan or container.
4. Once the oil has stopped dripping, tighten the oil drain plug to the specified torque (30 ft-lbs).

#### Refilling the Oil
1. Use a funnel to pour in the new oil.
2. Refer to your owner's manual for the recommended oil type and capacity.
3. Replace the oil filter with a new one.
4. Start the engine and check for any leaks.

### Filter Replacement
#### Air Filter Replacement
1. Locate the air filter housing, which is usually located in the engine compartment.
2. Use a 12mm socket to loosen the air filter housing screws.
3. Remove the air filter and discard it.
4. Install a new air filter and tighten the housing screws to the specified torque (10 ft-lbs).

#### Fuel Filter Replacement
1. Locate the fuel filter, which is usually located along the fuel line.
2. Use a fuel filter wrench to loosen the fuel filter.
3. Remove the fuel filter and discard it.
4. Install a new fuel filter and tighten it to the specified torque (10 ft-lbs).

### Turbocharger Inspection
#### Removing the Turbocharger
1. Locate the turbocharger, which is usually located on the driver's side of the engine.
2. Use a 10mm socket to loosen the turbocharger mounting bolts.
3. Remove the turbocharger and inspect it for any signs of damage or wear.
4. Use a rag to clean the turbocharger and its surrounding components.

#### Reinstalling the Turbocharger
1. Reinstall the turbocharger and tighten the mounting bolts to the specified torque (30 ft-lbs).
2. Use a torque wrench to ensure the bolts are tightened evenly and securely.
3. Start the engine and check for any leaks or unusual noises.

### Coil Spring Replacement
#### Removing the Coil Spring
1. Locate the coil spring, which is usually located in the front suspension.
2. Use a 12mm socket to loosen the coil spring mounting bolts.
3. Remove the coil spring and discard it.
4. Use a rag to clean the coil spring mounting area.

#### Reinstalling the Coil Spring
1. Reinstall the coil spring and tighten the mounting bolts to the specified torque (40 ft-lbs).
2. Use a torque wrench to ensure the bolts are tightened evenly and securely.
3. Start the engine and check for any unusual noises or vibrations.
