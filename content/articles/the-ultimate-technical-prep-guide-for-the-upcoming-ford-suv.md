The new Ford Hybrid SUV is expected to feature a combination of a conventional internal combustion engine and an electric motor. The hybrid system will likely include a battery pack, an electric motor, and a power control unit. The suspension system may feature a MacPherson strut front suspension and a torsion beam rear suspension, providing a comfortable ride and stable handling.

Common failure points in hybrid vehicles include the battery pack, electric motor, and power control unit. ## Step-by-Step Guide
### Step 1: Suspension Inspection
#### Step 1.1: Visual Inspection
Inspect the suspension components, including the bushings, shocks, and struts, for signs of wear and tear. Look for cracks, rust, or corrosion on the suspension arms and control arms.

#### Step 1.2: Ride Height Check
Check the ride height of the vehicle to ensure it's within the manufacturer's specifications. Use a tape measure to measure the distance between the center of the wheel hub and the fender lip.

### Step 2: Electrical System Check
#### Step 2.1: Battery Check
Check the battery voltage and state of charge using a multimeter. Ensure the battery is fully charged and the voltage is within the manufacturer's specifications.

#### Step 2.2: Electrical System Scan
Use a scan tool to check the electrical system for any fault codes or warnings. Address any issues promptly to prevent damage to the system.

### Step 3: Hybrid System Maintenance
#### Step 3.1: Battery Pack Maintenance
Check the battery pack for any signs of wear and tear. Ensure the battery pack is properly secured and the connections are clean and tight.

#### Step 3.2: Electric Motor Maintenance
Check the electric motor for any signs of wear and tear. Ensure the motor is properly lubricated and the connections are clean and tight.

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes
