## Step-by-Step Guide
### Preparation and Inspection
#### Checking the Battery Management System
1. Locate the battery management system (BMS) module, usually found in the trunk or under the rear seat.
2. Connect a multimeter to the BMS module and measure the voltage and current output.
3. Compare the readings to the manufacturer's specifications and update the BMS software if necessary.

#### Inspecting the Brake Pads
1. Raise the vehicle using a lift or jack stands and remove the wheels.
2. Inspect the brake pads for wear and tear, looking for signs of excessive wear or damage.
3. Replace the brake pads if necessary, following the manufacturer's recommendations.

#### Aligning the Wheels
1. Use a wheel alignment tool to measure the camber, toe, and ride height.
2. Adjust the suspension and steering components as needed to achieve the recommended alignment specifications.

### Maintenance and Optimization
#### Updating the Vehicle's Software
1. Connect a computer or tablet to the vehicle's diagnostic port.
2. Download and install the latest software updates from the manufacturer's website.
3. Follow the on-screen instructions to complete the update process.

#### Checking and Maintaining the Tires
1. Inspect the tires for proper inflation, tread depth, and wear patterns.
2. Rotate the tires every 5,000 to 7,500 miles to ensure even wear and extend their lifespan.
3. Balance the tires to prevent vibrations and uneven wear.

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes
* Avoid overcharging the battery, as this can reduce its lifespan and affect the vehicle's performance.
* Use the correct type and amount of lubricant for the vehicle's suspension and steering components.
* Do not attempt to repair or replace high-voltage electrical components without proper training and equipment.
