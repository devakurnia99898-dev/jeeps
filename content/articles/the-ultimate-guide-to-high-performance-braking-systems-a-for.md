## Technical Deep Dive
A high-performance braking system consists of several key components, including:
* Brake calipers: Responsible for clamping the brake pads onto the rotor
* Brake rotors: The metal discs that the brake pads contact to slow the vehicle
* Brake pads: The friction material that contacts the rotor to slow the vehicle
* Master cylinder: Converts non-hydraulic pressure from the brake pedal into hydraulic pressure to apply the brakes
* Brake fluid: Transfers pressure from the master cylinder to the brake calipers

Common failure points in braking systems include:
* Warped or excessively worn brake rotors
* Overheated or degraded brake pads
* Leaks in the brake fluid system
* Corrosion or damage to the brake calipers or master cylinder

## Step-by-Step Guide
### Step 1: Inspect the Braking System
#### Step 1.1: Visual Inspection
Inspect the brake calipers, rotors, and pads for signs of wear or damage. Check for any leaks in the brake fluid system and ensure the master cylinder is functioning properly.

#### Step 1.2: Brake Fluid Check
Check the brake fluid level and condition. If the fluid is dirty or low, it may be necessary to flush and replace it.

### Step 2: Upgrade the Brake Rotors
#### Step 2.1: Remove the Wheels and Calipers
Remove the wheels and calipers to access the brake rotors. Use a 10mm socket to loosen the caliper bolts and a 12mm socket to remove the rotor retaining screws.

#### Step 2.2: Remove the Old Rotors
Carefully pull the old rotors off the hub assembly and set them aside.

#### Step 2.3: Install the New Rotors
Install the new rotors, ensuring they are properly seated and secured with the retaining screws. Tighten the screws to the specified torque (typically 50-70 ft-lbs).

### Step 3: Upgrade the Brake Pads
#### Step 3.1: Remove the Old Pads
Remove the old brake pads from the calipers. Use a screwdriver to pry the pads out of the caliper.

#### Step 3.2: Install the New Pads
Install the new brake pads, ensuring they are properly seated and secured in the caliper. Apply a thin layer of brake lubricant to the back of the pads.

### Step 4: Bleed the Brake System
#### Step 4.1: Attach the Bleeding Kit
Attach the bleeding kit to the brake caliper and pump the brake pedal several times to pressurize the system.

#### Step 4.2: Bleed the System
Open the bleeding valve and allow the brake fluid to flow out. Repeat this process for each brake caliper, working from the furthest caliper from the master cylinder.

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes
* Always use high-quality brake components and follow proper installation procedures to ensure safety and performance.
* Avoid mixing different types of brake fluid, as this can cause compatibility issues and lead to system failure.
* Don't neglect to bleed the brake system after upgrading the brake components, as this can lead to spongy brake pedals and reduced stopping power.
