## The Diagnostic / The Situation
The 12-volt battery in modern hybrid vehicles is a critical component that powers the vehicle's accessories, including the smart entry and electric door release systems. However, repeated 12-volt battery failures have been reported in certain models, such as the Lexus LBX and Toyota Yaris Cross, leaving owners stranded. It is essential to understand the technical background and symptoms of 12-volt battery failures to diagnose and replace the battery effectively.

* Electric door releases not functioning

## Technical Deep Dive
The 12-volt battery in hybrid vehicles is a maintenance-free battery that is designed to last the lifetime of the vehicle. However, factors such as extreme temperatures, deep discharging, and manufacturing defects can cause the battery to fail prematurely. The 12-volt battery is connected to the vehicle's electrical system, which includes the alternator, starter motor, and various accessories.

Common failure points in the 12-volt battery system include:
* The battery itself, which can suffer from internal corrosion, sulfation, or manufacturing defects
* The battery terminals, which can become corroded or loose
* The alternator, which can fail to charge the battery properly
* The starter motor, which can draw excessive current and drain the battery

## Step-by-Step Guide
### Step 1: Prepare the Vehicle
#### Step 1.1: Locate the 12-Volt Battery
The 12-volt battery is usually located in the engine compartment or in the trunk. Consult the vehicle's manual to determine the exact location.

#### Step 1.2: Disconnect the Negative Battery Terminal
Use a 10mm socket to loosen the negative battery terminal bolt. Then, pull the terminal off the battery post.

### Step 2: Test the 12-Volt Battery
#### Step 2.1: Use a Multimeter to Measure the Battery Voltage
Set the multimeter to DC voltage and measure the battery voltage. A fully charged 12-volt battery should read around 12.6 volts.

#### Step 2.2: Use a Battery Tester to Check the Battery's State of Charge
A battery tester can help determine the battery's state of charge and identify any potential problems.

### Step 3: Replace the 12-Volt Battery
#### Step 3.1: Remove the Old Battery
Use a torque wrench to loosen the battery hold-down clamp. Then, pull the old battery out of the compartment.

#### Step 3.2: Install the New Battery
Place the new battery in the compartment and secure it with the hold-down clamp. Tighten the clamp to the specified torque (usually around 10-15 Nm).

#### Step 3.3: Connect the Negative Battery Terminal
Place the negative battery terminal back on the battery post and tighten the bolt to the specified torque (usually around 10-15 Nm).

### Step 4: Test the Electrical System
#### Step 4.1: Turn On the Ignition
Turn the ignition switch to the "on" position and check the dashboard warning lights.

#### Step 4.2: Test the Smart Entry and Electric Door Release Systems
Test the smart entry and electric door release systems to ensure they are functioning properly.

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes
* Always use a torque wrench to tighten the battery terminals and hold-down clamp
* Avoid over-tightening the battery terminals, which can damage the battery posts
* Use a multimeter to measure the battery voltage and ensure it is within the specified range
* Don't forget to disconnect the negative battery terminal before starting work

## Frequently Asked Questions
1. **Q: What are the symptoms of a 12-volt battery failure?**
A: Symptoms of 12-volt battery failures include failure of the smart entry system, electric door releases not functioning, warning lights on the dashboard, and difficulty starting the engine.
2. **Q: How often should I replace my 12-volt battery?**
A: The 12-volt battery in hybrid vehicles is designed to last the lifetime of the vehicle. However, it is recommended to replace the battery every 5-7 years or as specified in the vehicle's manual.
3. **Q: Can I replace the 12-volt battery myself?**
A: Yes, replacing the 12-volt battery is a relatively simple process that can be done at home with basic tools and knowledge.
4. **Q: What type of 12-volt battery should I use?**
A: It is recommended to use a maintenance-free 12-volt battery that is specifically designed for hybrid vehicles.
5. **Q: How do I properly dispose of my old 12-volt battery?**
A: It is recommended to take the old 12-volt battery to a recycling center or an auto parts store that accepts battery recycling. Do not dispose of the battery in the trash or landfill.
