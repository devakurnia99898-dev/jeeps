## The Diagnostic / The Situation
The J.D. Power U.S. Automotive Performance, Execution, and Layout (APEAL) Study is an annual study that focuses on how owners feel about their new vehicles after the first 90 days of ownership. Rather than simply counting mechanical problems, J.D. Power evaluates 37 attributes across 10 vehicle categories to determine which vehicles are the most appealing to their owners. As a Jeep owner, understanding the factors that contribute to a vehicle's APEAL score can help you maximize your Jeep's performance, execution, and layout.

Symptoms or signs to look for in a vehicle with low APEAL score include poor performance, uncomfortable ride, unresponsive handling, and lack of features. If you're experiencing any of these issues with your Jeep, it may be time to take a closer look at the vehicle's performance, execution, and layout.

## Technical Deep Dive
The performance, execution, and layout of a Jeep are determined by a combination of factors, including the engine, transmission, suspension, and electrical systems. The engine, for example, is the heart of the vehicle, providing the power and torque needed to propel the vehicle forward. The transmission, on the other hand, is responsible for transferring that power to the wheels. The suspension system, including the shocks, struts, and springs, works to absorb bumps and provide a smooth ride. Finally, the electrical system, including the battery, starter, and alternator, provides the power needed to run the vehicle's accessories.

Common failure points in these systems include the water pump, which can leak and cause overheating, the spark plugs, which can foul and cause poor performance, and the suspension bushings, which can wear out and cause a rough ride.

## Step-by-Step Guide
### Step 1: Perform Routine Maintenance
Performing routine maintenance is essential to maximizing your Jeep's performance, execution, and layout. This includes regular oil changes, tire rotations, and brake pad replacements.

#### Step 1.1: Check the Owner's Manual
Check the owner's manual to determine the recommended maintenance schedule for your Jeep.

#### Step 1.2: Change the Oil
Change the oil every 5,000 to 7,500 miles, depending on the type of oil used.

#### Step 1.3: Rotate the Tires
Rotate the tires every 6,000 to 8,000 miles to ensure even wear.

### Step 2: Upgrade the Suspension
Upgrading the suspension can improve the ride and handling of your Jeep.

#### Step 2.1: Choose the Right Suspension
Choose a suspension system that is designed for your Jeep's make and model.

#### Step 2.2: Install the New Suspension
Install the new suspension system, following the manufacturer's instructions.

### Step 3: Improve the Electrical System
Improving the electrical system can provide more power to the vehicle's accessories.

#### Step 3.1: Upgrade the Battery
Upgrade the battery to a high-performance battery.

#### Step 3.2: Install a High-Performance Alternator
Install a high-performance alternator to provide more power to the electrical system.

## Pro Tips & Common Mistakes
* Don't overload the vehicle, as this can cause poor performance and reduced fuel efficiency.
* Don't neglect routine maintenance, as this can lead to costly repairs down the road.
* Do consider upgrading the suspension and electrical systems to improve the ride and handling of your Jeep.
